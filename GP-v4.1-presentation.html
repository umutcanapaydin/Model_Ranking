01 — Cover

General Pipeline v4.1

A versioned, evidence-ratified methodology for building software with AI agents. This is not a product pitch — it is the technical record of what happened across fourteen versions: what worked, what broke, and what we found broken in our own shipped package.

Audience: developers who have shipped for years. No softening of terms. A glossary comes first, because our own notation (V4C-13, A0.5, ⛔ glob) is unreadable from outside this project.

02 — Glossary · 1 of 2 · notation

What does "V4C-13" actually mean?

Every rule carries an identity, and that identity points back to evidence. The rule text may change; the identity never does. We abbreviate in conversation, but there is always a record behind the token.

TokenWhat it meansExample

GDF General Distributed Flow — the delegated lane. Agents merge themselves behind mechanical gates; the human reviews asynchronously. Separate repository, separate version line. v1.1, not yet piloted
V3C-nn
V4C-nn		 Candidate rule. V3C-01..107 in the v3 series, V4C-01.. in v4. Numbers are never reused. A candidate is adopted, deferred with a trigger, or rejected — and the outcome is recorded either way. V4C-13 = friction budget
P-00x Process ADR — an architecture decision record about the process itself. Project-level ADRs use D-1xx, so the two can never be confused. P-005 = risk-tiered review

GDF-00x The GDF lane's own decision records (GDF-001..009). GDF-009 = Jira provides no lock

04 — Mental model

In thirty seconds: two lanes, one constitution

GP — owner in the loop

  • Clear PRD, production-critical work
  • Owner reviews at every milestone, runs his own tests, makes all commits
  • Agents never run git in this lane
  • Operating mode: A0.5 (PROVISIONAL)

GDF — delegated

  • Fuzzy PRD, speed matters, not production-critical
  • A PM agent orchestrates via Jira and is READ-ONLY on the repository
  • Builder agents merge — protected branches, CI, and a risk-tiered review bench as required status checks
  • Backpressure caps, tripwires, auto-freeze

The shared constitution lives in GP; GDF references it by pin and never copies it. GDF's deviations sit in a one-page override appendix. If that appendix ever grows past a page, that is the drift alarm.

The third layer, and the actual differentiator: the process itself evolves by evidence. Every project files a structured experience report → findings enter the register → a blind council ratifies → a version is cut. Every rule in the package traces back to a real incident, and the trail is readable.

05 — The flow

Five stages plus a maintenance loop

STAGE 0 BOOTSTRAP make bootstrap-check until green · licence gate · no stray placeholders ↓ STAGE 1 PLAN milestone plan · acceptance criteria + a citing test named per criterion · risk tiers ↓ STAGE 2 WAVE parallel agents · each runs its own dev-test loop (implement→test→self-review→fix) ↓ STAGE 3 REVIEW fresh eyes: Code-Reviewer + Tester (one combined seat at LOW/MED, both at HIGH) Tester fault injection: break it → confirm RED → revert byte-identical, md5-verified a fault that stays GREEN = a missing test = a mandatory new test ↓ wave-close checklist filled and committed (make wave-check) STAGE 4 CLOSURE 4.0 security review (BLOCKING) → 4.1 quality gate → 4.2 capture → 4.3 deploy + go-live (cold-start, check-templates, journey) → 4.4 handoff THE OWNER'S SESSION: his own tests · the diffs · every commit ↓ STAGE 5 MAINTENANCE production bug → red-test intake → fix wave → fixpack → owner out-of-sandbox verification (BLOCKING) → deploy → watch window → the lesson lands in EXPERIENCE.md as a deploy condition

Stage 5 was added in v3.4 because our first production project ran roughly five fix deploys ad hoc after go-live — the five stages simply ended at go-live. A textbook case of noticing what is missing only after it has already cost you.

06 — The council

How a version gets ratified

Per three independent market scans, this is the mechanism with no equivalent found anywhere: a blind, multi-role AI council that ratifies changes to the process. Jury and debate literature judges outputs; nothing in product or research ratifies a process version.

1. HARVEST field report / research → the register, as a numbered increment 2. TRIAGE the chair gives each candidate an ID, its source, the files it touches, a requested weight 3. BRIEF every seat gets the same candidates, its own lens, and the binding evidence rules 4. BLIND VOTE seats ballot in parallel, none seeing another's ballot 5. TALLY the chair counts; weight demotions and conditions are applied 6. SPLIT a tie or a genuine split goes to THE OWNER (who may delegate it back to the chair) 7. MINUTES ratification.md — dissents recorded verbatim, never summarised away 8. CUT package copied forward (superset), adopts placed into files, overview regenerated LAST

The verdict set (extended in v4.0)

VerdictMeaning
ADOPTAccepted as written.
ADOPT-WITH-CONDITIONSAccepted, but every condition names an owner, a date and a closure artifact. A condition without an artifact evaporates.
DEFERDeferred — always with a re-table trigger. An open-ended deferral is a silent status-quo adoption.
REJECTRejected, with the reason on the record.

EVIDENCE		Abstention. Why it exists: a seat that cannot abstain will fabricate a plausible-looking verdict — precisely the failure mode this whole apparatus is built to catch. Silence or a timeout counts as this, and never as approval.

The rule's first live application happened in the very session that ratified it: a seat omitted one candidate from its ballot, and the omission was counted as insufficient evidence rather than assent.

07 — Anti-bloat

Why zero new gates for six consecutive cuts

Because there is a well-known way a methodology dies: adding a rule looks free, and accumulating rules is fatal. The countermeasures are structural, not cultural:

  • Weight can be demoted, never promoted. No seat may say "make this a gate" — and neither may the chair. Promotion to gate weight is a separate vote in a later round.
  • A hard budget: at most fifteen adopts per round. In v4.0 the Skeptic deliberately stopped at fourteen: "a methodology that maxes its own budget every batch has no budget."
  • Checklist cap: the wave-close checklist is capped at twelve rows; adding a row requires naming the incident that triggered it, and one-in-one-out is preferred.
  • House-rules diet: AGENTS.md has a 150-line hard cap (currently 140). Detail is pushed down into .agents/rules/.
  • Schema narrowness (V4C-35, new in v4.1): a field may exist only if a check consumes it; every required field must answer "which concrete failure does its absence permit?"; fields unused for two cuts are deleted.
  • Friction budget (V4C-13): every control carries a minutes-when-it-fires cost line; a control skipped under pressure is recorded as a first-class finding; the same control skipped three times triggers a review of the control, not the people.

In v4.1 the streak ended on purpose: the council adopted the project's first real gate, seven votes to two. The Skeptic's reasoning: "one declared gate is worth more than eight guardrails that quietly block."

10 — Timeline · v3.3 → v3.4

A cadence change, then closing the lifecycle

v3.3 · 91 files · OD-4 · 0 gates

The owner's directive: "I will carry the human review between miles, not between waves." That became mode A0.5 (PROVISIONAL): waves close agent-side — fresh-eyes reviews, green checks pinned to the closing tree, and a committed checklist — while the owner reviews at every milestone in a sixty-to-ninety-minute session, runs his own tests, and makes the commits.

The safety valves matter more than the change: milestones are capped at four to six waves or about 2,000 net lines; the owner makes a labelled checkpoint commit per wave (wip: NOT reviewed); the escalate-NOW list interrupts mid-wave; an assumption ledger stays active; and an automatic reversion tripwire fires if the new cadence ever misses something the old one would have caught. The bright line: an agent committing to main is A1, and A1 requires an explicit owner ADR. There is no other path to it.

v3.4 · 93 files · OD-6 · 0 gates · STAGE 5

The evidence: our first production project ran roughly five fix deploys after go-live, and the process ended at go-live. The answer was written as reuse by the Skeptic's ruling, not as new machinery:

  • Red-test intake: a fix wave cannot open until the production bug is reproduced as a failing test. The red test is the frozen spec; a fix wave only turns red green and never adds behaviour.
  • The fixpack is the deploy gate: at most five fixes or about 400 lines (a HIGH-risk fix ships alone), a security floor, full regression on the exact bundled build, and a migration plus rollback plan.
  • Owner out-of-sandbox verification (BLOCKING): reproduce the bug on the pre-fix build, confirm it is gone on the fixpack build, run local tests, sign. No signature, no deploy.
  • Mechanical capture: fixpack lessons append to EXPERIENCE.md as a deploy condition. Memory-based harvesting was retired — because the same experience file was uploaded byte-identical three times, which proved the discipline had already broken.
11 — Timeline · v3.5 — the best evidence we have

Seven defects, zero caught by gates, one hundred percent boundary defects

Our first real post-production dataset: a customer-operated, air-gapped Kubernetes cluster with a partner-owned build pipeline. After "build complete" — nine of nine milestones, every gate green — seven defects.

0caught by automated gates
100%boundary defects
563unit tests, all correctly green
3found by the first real deployment

The doctrine that came out of it: our gates were excellent at the correctness of what we imagined, and blind to the shape of the world we deployed into. A larger suite of the same kind of test would have caught none of them. The defect classes were all seams: code against its own config · code against packaging · wave against wave · code against the human operator · tooling against reality · process against the shape of the phase.

The answer — outward-facing checks:

  • make check-templates — every shipped config template must actually instantiate the settings parser. This is the docs-against-code config contract, and two total outages came from breaking it.
  • make cold-start — boot against an empty datastore and assert the service reaches serve-ready, or says not-ready honestly.
  • Human-path criterion — a person who did not build the system must be able to obtain a credential and make an authorized call using only the shipped documentation. Automated testers mint tokens internally and can never see this gap. That defect hid for about four weeks and the first human to try found it immediately.
  • Readiness is not liveness — /ready must actually probe its dependency and return 503 when it is down; /health stays dependency-free. And a fail-closed control must emit an operator-actionable reason: correct behaviour that is indistinguishable from a bug is a diagnosability defect, not a success.
  • Black-box journey tester — a standard-library script that walks the real customer journey against the deployed URL. It became the single most valuable artifact of the support phase.
  • Producer enumeration — when a wave hardens a shared invariant, every producer of that invariant's inputs is enumerated from the code and each one requires its own citing test. Otherwise the seam between two individually-correct waves is unowned, which is exactly where one of the seven defects lived.

Bonus validation: the field team independently derived the fixpack shape without having seen v3.4 — the strongest convergence this project has recorded.

The council audited the repository, not the briefing — and found that controls we had already ratified did not work

I convened nine seats for the architecture round. Seven of the nine inspected the live tree instead of voting on the briefing they were handed. I then verified every finding myself, with the commands in the record:

#4 — The enforcement point did not exist at all

The GP repository root has no .github/workflows/. Every workflow lives inside a general_pipeline_vX/ package, because those are templates for projects that copy the package. The mechanism worked on its author. That became the strongest single argument in the batch for machine-checked records.

15 — v4.1 · Phase 1

Records became data — and the validator itself got tested

Three independent architecture passes reached the same conclusion: do not build an engine. The Temporal, Argo, Prefect and Hatchet class is a reject at our scale, and the deciding argument is not licence cost but failure ownership: "a service you run at two in the morning, alone, is a liability." None of them addresses our actual failure mode either — which is not "a process crashed" but "a step was skipped and nothing noticed".

And the uncomfortable positive finding: we are not behind on this axis; the field simply never built the validator. AGENTS.md across 60,000-plus repositories, Spec Kit at 124,300 stars, and Kiro's spec workflow — all three have zero machine enforcement, and AGENTS.md says so in its own FAQ. The real prior art is not engines at all: ethereum/EIPs with its rule file, kubernetes/enhancements with kep.yaml plus a validator, and python/peps with one standard-library script.

17 — GDF · the delegated lane and a correctness defect

There was no such thing as an "atomic claim"

GDF's design said: "claiming is a Jira transition plus assignee in one API call; the transition is the lock." The architecture research challenged that, flagged it as its own weakest claim, and asked for verification before anyone acted on it. I verified it — and the finding is worse than the report stated:

$ GET https://jira.atlassian.com/browse/JRASERVER-26005

"As a program, I can opt into some form of optimistic locking
 when updating an issue via REST"

Type:        Suggestion
Resolution:  Won't Do
Resolved:    25/Jun/2015
Related:     JRACLOUD-26005  —  Closed

# The report called it an open feature request. In fact Atlassian CONSIDERED conditional
# update eleven years ago and DECLINED it. There is no If-Match precondition and there will
# not be one; the documented idiom remains GET → modify → PUT.
# Two builders read "unassigned", both write, and the last write wins — SILENTLY.

What was done (GDF-009)

  • The false claim was struck from both files. The mandatory read-back stays, but it is now named for what it is: it narrows the race window, it does not close it. "If the read-back shows another assignee, STOP — do not try to win the race."
  • Interim operating rule: one builder per domain, until the lease pilot passes.
  • The git-ref claim lease is a PILOT — novel, and unvalidated by anyone's experience. git push origin HEAD:refs/claims/<TICKET> is a genuine server-side compare-and-swap: exactly one pusher wins. Preconditions before it may be relied on: a two-racer test on the real host, force-push and delete forbidden on that ref namespace, a lease expiry with a CI-only sweeper, and an attempt counter that escalates.

The lesson

This defect was live in an unpiloted design, and it was found by audit rather than by production. The GDF design council, the market round and the v1.1 council all missed it; the architecture research caught it, and only because the report was honest enough to flag its own weakest claim.

It became a standing rule: verify every guarantee you assume a platform provides, against the primary document. The word "atomic" is free in a design document. It is not free in a REST API.

18 — What we refuse to build

docs/refusals.md — a refusal is a decision too

A refusal that is not written down gets re-litigated every quarter. Eleven refusals, each with a reason and a re-open trigger. The council called it unanimously "the highest return per line in the batch".

RefusedWhy, at our scale (one owner plus agents, no platform team, no on-call rotation)
Any workflow or durable-execution engine
Temporal · Restate · Hatchet · Argo · Prefect · Dagster		 Failure ownership, not licence cost. And none of them addresses our real failure mode: a step was skipped and nothing noticed.
Durable execution's replay model It requires determinism, and LLM steps are not deterministic. Copying the mechanism without its precondition yields a system that looks resumable and silently diverges. We keep the salvageable half: run identifiers and an append-only journal.
Any new configuration or policy language
CUE · Pkl · Dhall · Rego		 Our operators are language models, and fluency is an operational requirement. A schema an agent can author and repair unassisted beats a more expressive schema it will subtly get wrong twice a year, debugged by an owner who last saw the language six months ago.
A memory or vector service for exact state Approximate retrieval answers "what might be relevant"; governance state needs "which record is at which stage, and what was decided". For a corpus of dozens of markdown files, grep is more precise and free.
Any hosted trace backend that receives transcripts That means shipping source code — and, through error output, sometimes credentials — to a third party, in a system whose entire credential design is "CI holds them". Metrics and event names are fine; trace bodies are not.
Any low-star tool on a governance path Measured: adr-tools at 5,500 stars has been dead since 2020; log4brains is dormant; kyverno-json at 93 stars is dormant. Meanwhile MADR, JSON Schema, AGENTS.md, markdown and git are all alive and will outlive every CLI built on them. Adopt formats; write your own 150 lines.
Semantic versions derived from commit messages It lets an agent's choice of words bump the constitution's major version. Version semantics are the owner's decision, made deliberately.
Rung 5 of the maturity ladder
an executed, stateful, traced governance engine		 Refused by decision, not deferred by budget. No published project governs its own process on such an engine; rung 5 is where vendors build products, not where anyone governs a process. The rule: do not climb a rung merely because the ladder has another rung.

20 — Takeaways

Portable lessons, independent of this methodology

1 · A declared control that silently passes is worse than an absent one

Because you believe you bought protection from it. Ours never ran across two cuts and reported green the entire time. You must have watched every control go red at least once, or you do not have it.

2 · Validate the validator

Negative fixtures, declared expected diagnostics, and a self-test in CI. If you apply fault injection to your code, apply it to your controls. Ours found the validator's own bug on the first run.

3 · A skipped required job reports SUCCESS

On GitHub, conditional required checks are advisory in disguise. One unconditional aggregate check, bound to the intended app, bypass disabled, and the workflow path itself protected. Otherwise you do not have a gate, you have a habit.

4 · Verify platform guarantees from the primary source

"Atomic claim" is free in a design document. In Jira it does not exist, and the vendor declined to add it in 2015. Assumed guarantees are the main source of intermittent failures that hide for months.

5 · A refusal is a decision — write it down

refusals.md ends the quarterly re-litigation. Every entry carries a reason and a re-open trigger. Not "not yet", but "no, until this specific thing becomes true".

6 · Separate your evidence classes and say so

Field data is not literature, and literature is not a vendor claim. Binding for us: literature corroborates, it never gates. A single-source vendor number can never carry guardrail weight.

7 · A reviewer that cannot abstain will fabricate

Without an explicit insufficient-evidence verdict, AI seats produce plausible-looking judgements. And silence or a timeout is never assent — it is an escalation.

8 · Whoever adds a rule writes its cost

Every control states its minutes when it fires, who may bypass it, and where the bypass gets recorded. A friction budget that is never spent is not a budget — it is decoration.

21 — What's next

Open work and honest unknowns

Immediately — owner action

  • Make governance-contract a required check on the protected branch, bind it to the intended app, disable bypass, and protect the workflow path with CODEOWNERS. Until that is done the gate is advisory — which is precisely this cut's own lesson pointed back at us.
  • Hand the delegated lane to its own agent; the handover document is written and waiting.

Measurement window — through 2026-10-30

  • Phase 2 does not start: presence gate, pre-commit harness, the shared SHA-pinned workflow, permission reconciliation, and the six-field resume contract all wait.
  • Increment 12 is gated until friction and council telemetry each report once.
  • Quarterly differentiator-ledger review, plus the standing market re-scan.

Honest unknowns

  • Nobody publishes the measured benefit of governance validation. Our own before-and-after will be the only evidence that exists on the question.
  • The delegated lane has never run a pilot. Eight rules now sit on a machine that has never turned over — the Skeptic's ceiling: if no pilot starts, the next round defaults to reject on every pre-pilot candidate.
  • Autonomy levels A1 and A2 are not active, and cannot become active without an explicit owner decision record.
  • A head start is not a moat. GitHub owns both halves of the trust-telemetry mechanic; they are simply not wired together yet.

Chartered theme: process engine

Candidate status, not adopted. A generalisation of the doctrine "declared means machine-checked": machine-readable constitution and config schemas, resumable state, schema-validated records, and agent-run tracing. Like the agent-native theme chartered back in v3, it waits for its evidence instead of being built on enthusiasm.

The defensible part of this methodology is not a feature list. It is that decisions are bound to evidence, dissent is never deleted, and controls test themselves. Everything else is copyable — and it will be copied.— the chair
