1. WHAT CHANGED IN THIS QUARTER, AND WHY IT MATTERS MORE THAN THE FEATURES
=======================================================================
M10 shipped a router. M11 made the reviewer somebody else and executed 1,093 lines of Swift that
nothing had ever run. M12 made the product usable by a person who does not work here.

The features are not the story. The story is that in this quarter the project STOPPED REVIEWING
ITSELF, and the results were immediate and uncomfortable:

  * D-133 (M11-W1) — K.7 had described a situation that did not exist in a single-agent lane, so
    the author reviewed their own work four times, each declared, each closed green. Now the
    reviewing seat is a separate session that reads policy from the protected base ref and leaves
    a FILE. **Three seats ran under the new rule at M11 and all three returned BLOCKING — and every
    blocking finding was in the machinery built to enforce a rule, never in the served product.**
  * The M11 council (four seats, two of them chairs the owner added) found ONE defect with eight
    instances: a thing asserted somewhere and exercised nowhere, each half locally correct. A
    product calling itself budget-aware with no budget control. An ADR cited in 30 files that had
    never been written. A counter that had never once fired while two records claimed it had.
  * M12 was planned entirely from what four seats and one CFO said.

**What independent review bought was not better code. It was the discovery that the controls were
weaker than the records said.**

=======================================================================
2. THE DEFECT CLASS THIS PROJECT KEEPS PAYING FOR
=======================================================================
Every milestone in this quarter found the same shape, and it is worth learning before you touch
anything:

    A thing is asserted in one place and exercised in none, and BOTH HALVES ARE LOCALLY CORRECT.

The code is right about what it does. The record is right about something. The gap exists only
across the boundary between them, and no review that looks at one side can see it. Instances:

  * `D-120` — "a K.8 frozen contract, so the SET is pinned" (a test's words). Never written.
  * `budget = "unlimited"` — hardcoded, while `/v1/budgets`, `eligible_count` and a rendered
    "25 fit your budget" all served it.
  * `C2b` — 22 accepted rows, 22 unique keys, zero triggers ever, two records asserting it fired.
  * `MIN_QUALITY_PCT == 65.0` — a literal compared to itself in the module that defines it, while
    the engine reads `spec.min_quality`.
  * Code signing "skipped for the Simulator" — `DEVELOPMENT_TEAM` appears zero times; the device
    build has never once succeeded.
  * `ORDERING_NOTE` — "ordered alphabetically", true when written and false from the moment the
    client began putting the selected surface first.

If you find yourself writing "as X guarantees" in a comment, go and check that X does.

=======================================================================
3. THE TESTING LESSON, WHICH IS NOT THE ONE YOU EXPECT
=======================================================================
This suite is large and takes testing seriously, and its recurring failure is not laziness. It is
**a fixture that cannot reach what it asserts**, in progressively subtler forms:

  * data — a threshold fixture whose prices are nowhere near the cap (M11 tester seat: 22% kill
    rate on the nine surfaces' calibrations, against 71% on the engine);
  * ENVIRONMENT — the Turkish locale fix passed four behavioural tests with `Locale.current`
    restored, because this machine folds like English. Only asserting the CHOICE killed it;
  * LOCATION — three tests in two waves went red on changes that kept every word of their intent,
    because they pinned an offset (a literal phrase, a 600-character window, a grep for a
    comparison that had moved into a tested function).

The rule that came out of it: **pin the claim and the audience, never the offset.** A test that
fails when text moves teaches people to leave text where it is.

4. THE SMALLEST AMOUNT OF RUNNING WORTH ADDING TO A CLOSE
=======================================================================
M10 asked this. M11 answered it and the answer was not close.

Executing code found real defects and they were worth their cost — a `runner` section calling
commands that do not exist, a Make recipe that passed on zero tests, a CI workflow that had run
twice in its life and failed both times because it was structurally unpassable.

**A person using the app for twenty minutes found six defects that eleven milestones of gates had
not**, because every one of them is about what a reader CONCLUDES from what they can see. Then five
requirements arrived that nobody inside the project would have written.

M12 then asked what could be MEASURED to catch that earlier, and the honest answer is: not much.
A jargon gate exists and catches the words you already regret. The 28-row inventory that drove the
whole milestone came from a seat told to read the product as a stranger and given no other job.
**You cannot gate comprehension, but you can staff it.**

=======================================================================
5. GATES — WHAT RUNS, AND WHAT HAD BEEN LYING
=======================================================================
`make check` runs ten legs including `swift-test` (new at M11) behind a COUNT FLOOR — because
`swift test` exits 0 having executed nothing, and an undiscovered suite would otherwise pass in
silence. That was the third gate-cannot-fail in one wave; the other two were a pipe swallowing an
exit code, and a `runner` section calling `pass`/`fail`, which are not commands in that script.

`make gate` exited 2 on a clean tree for months, and it is what the post-edit hook runs. Its own
log had never been written. Fixed at M12-W1.

`contract-tests.yml` had run TWICE in its life and failed both times — not from source drift, but
because it enforced the full suite's coverage floor on a step running 14 network tests. Structurally
unpassable from the day it was written, and because step 1 failed the three steps below it had
never executed once. **A badge that has always been red carries no information.**

`shellcheck` is NOT installed and NO gate lints any shell: `runner`, six scripts, `ios/app.sh`.
Offered to the owner at M11, DECLINED. It is W-074 and it is a decision, not an oversight.

7. THE QUESTION THIS QUARTER LEAVES
=======================================================================
Every wave of M12 discharged findings that came from outside the people doing the work, and it
produced the largest usability gain in twelve milestones.

**The work the team chose for itself, across eleven milestones, was correctness — and correctness
was never the thing standing between this product and a user.**

The council was convened once, by the owner, on a hunch. Decide whether an outside seat is a thing
this project HAS — scheduled, with a standing question — or a thing it reaches for when somebody
remembers.
